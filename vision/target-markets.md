# Target Markets

Events are for the people the adoption data shows are in motion, and whom CalMU or ThirdPlaces already reaches.

Employment since late 2022 has split inside the same occupations. Workers ages 22–25 in the most AI-exposed jobs have lost seats, and graduates of the most exposed majors are less likely to be hired. Older workers in those same jobs have gained seats. Employers are paying for AI skill and retraining the people they intend to keep. A third group sits underneath that split: people in roles where the forecasts expect employment to keep falling, whom CalMU already recruits as career changers.

- Experienced professionals in exposed occupations, who need to direct AI to stay in the work employers are keeping
- Students and new graduates, who need demonstrable AI skill to get hired into those industries
- Career changers leaving roles where employment is forecast to keep shrinking

## Experienced professionals

People with domain tenure in occupations where a large share of tasks is exposed: legal, finance, consulting, administration and operations, software, and other professional work. In the payroll data this is the older cohort inside those occupations.

**Why they are a market.** From late 2022 to September 2025, employment of older workers in the most AI-exposed occupations rose 6–9%, while employment of workers ages 22–25 in those same occupations fell about 6%. The work employers are keeping is judgment, clients, and exceptions. Domain experts who can direct the tools are the group still growing inside exposed occupations. In the World Economic Forum survey, two-thirds of employers plan to hire people with specific AI skills, and 85% plan to upskill the people they already have. PwC finds a 56% wage premium for AI skills inside the same occupation. Shopify has made effective AI use a condition of performance reviews and of any request for more headcount.

**What they come to do.** Learn a method they can apply to work they already own, and meet peers who use AI on that kind of work every day. The premium and the headcount rule attach to someone who can take a task through a tool and finish the part the tool cannot.

**Who already reaches them.** CalMU’s working professionals: business and technology programs taught by practitioner faculty, scheduled around a job, on campuses in San Diego, San Jose, and La Puente and online. ThirdPlaces is the condition for using AI on client and company conversations. Mobido leaves the choice of where that data goes — on the phone, on a home computer, or in the cloud — with the person whose work it is.

## Students and new graduates

CalMU students in business and technology, including international students in STEM-designated programs and veterans using education benefits, and near-graduates headed for the occupations where hiring has already tightened: software and information systems, and the business tracks that feed consulting, finance, and operations.

**Why they are a market.** The drop since late 2022 is at hiring. After firm-level controls, workers ages 22–25 in the most exposed quintile of jobs had about a 15 log-point relative employment decline versus the least exposed quintile. In the most AI-exposed college majors, the chance of a job soon after graduation fell 5 percentage points and starting earnings fell 13%, about half of that from graduates landing in lower-paid sectors such as retail and food service. The sharper executive forecasts name entry-level consulting, law, and finance. A conversational assistant raised productivity about 34% for novices in measured customer-support work, so the skill is learnable in the same window employers are cutting seats for people who only do the automatable tasks. Postings that ask for AI skills grew 7.5% while total postings fell 11.3%.

**What they come to do.** Leave with a practice they can show an employer, and with introductions to people who already work that way in the industries they are entering. For international students, that practice is what a STEM-designated degree has to look like in a hiring market that is narrowing Optional Practical Training seats.

**Who already reaches them.** CalMU’s student body: international students with visa support and STEM programs, veterans with credit for military training and help using education benefits, and business and technology degrees built around skills employers ask for. ThirdPlaces fits the daily habit the workshop is built on — an AI that reflects on ordinary conversation — with the record kept on a device they control.

## Career changers

Adults moving out of occupations where employment is forecast to keep falling, and using education to qualify for work that is still hiring. CalMU names them alongside students, working professionals, and veterans.

**Why they are a market.** McKinsey’s midpoint adoption scenario implies on the order of 12 million occupational transitions in the U.S. by 2030, about 7.5% of employment. Office support, customer service, and food service are where employment is expected to keep falling, while generative AI changes the task mix of STEM, creative, and professional jobs. In the World Economic Forum survey, 50% of employers expect to move people from declining roles into growing ones, and 63% name skill gaps as the barrier. This group’s aim is a hireable path into the roles experienced professionals are staying in.

**What they come to do.** See which skills the growing roles hire for, try those skills once, and find a next step into a program or a job. CalMU’s accelerated hybrid degrees in business and technology are the structured version of that move. Veterans using education benefits are a defined slice of this group.

**Who already reaches them.** CalMU recruits career changers directly, with flexible scheduling and practitioner faculty. ThirdPlaces matters after they start the new work: a private way to practice thinking with AI on everyday conversations while they are still building the habit employers now treat as part of the job.

## Where the events can draw a room

CalMU teaches from San Diego, San Jose, and La Puente, and online. A room event draws all three markets from those metros. San Jose concentrates software and professional exposure. San Diego and La Puente concentrate working professionals, veterans, and career changers who can attend on a weeknight. Online students and online professionals are the audience for what follows the event.

Across all three, ThirdPlaces sets a constraint on the offer. The workshop asks people to use AI on real work and real conversation. Mobido’s mission is that they choose the AI and where it runs, including fully on a phone or a home computer. Events that demonstrate tools should be able to show that choice.
