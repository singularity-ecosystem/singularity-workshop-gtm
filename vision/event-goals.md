# Event goals

A Singularity Workshop is for the three groups in the target markets: experienced professionals in exposed occupations, students and new graduates, and career changers. CalMU already recruits them from San Diego, San Jose, La Puente, and online. ThirdPlaces sets the condition on the tools. The evening works when each person uses a method on real work, meets someone who already works that way, and can see a next step with one of the partners.

## Outcomes

Every event produces three things.

**A method used on work the attendee already has.** Experienced professionals take a task they own through a tool and finish the part the tool cannot: judgment, clients, and exceptions. Students and new graduates leave with a practice they can show an employer. For international students, that practice is what a STEM-designated degree has to look like while Optional Practical Training seats narrow. Career changers try, once, a skill the growing roles hire for, and can name it afterward. A first event can open with a short picture of the tool landscape. That picture is there to set up the exercise.

**An introduction to someone in the same work.** Professionals meet peers who use AI on legal, finance, consulting, operations, or software work. Students meet people already hired into the industries they are entering. Career changers meet a practitioner or a program path. CalMU’s practitioner faculty and working students are the people in the room who can make that introduction specific.

**A next step the partners can keep.** Students, veterans, and career changers can see CalMU’s accelerated hybrid programs in business and technology as the structured version of the move. Working professionals can see a way to keep the practice around a job. Anyone who used a tool saw that they choose the AI and where it runs: on the phone with no internet, on a home computer they control, or in the cloud. Online students and online professionals are the audience for what follows the room.

## CalMU

CalMU’s mission is practical, technology-driven education that meets a student’s professional objective, taught by practitioner faculty, for students, working professionals, veterans, and career changers.

The event serves that mission when four things are true. The room is drawn from people the university already reaches, on a weeknight those campuses can fill. The teaching is a practitioner demonstrating a method on real work. Each of the three groups can tell which part of the evening is for them. The programs are a visible next step for people using education to qualify for work that is still hiring, including international students, veterans using education benefits, and career changers.

## ThirdPlaces

Mobido’s mission is that an AI listening to everyday conversation can help someone reflect, and that the person decides which AI does the listening and where the data lives. The workshop asks people to use AI on real work and real conversation, so that mission is a constraint on the offer.

The event serves the mission when the exercise is ordinary conversation or the attendee’s own work, and when a tool on stage can be shown under that choice: smartphone, home computer, or cloud. A company earns a place in the room when its tool helps with that work and can run where the person decides. Talks on trust, security, and a daily thought partner belong on the agenda because they are about that habit.

## December 10

The current description already contributes a level-set, a panel of daily practitioners, a curated showcase, and a long networking block.

Chris Heuer’s work on AI as a daily thought partner is the habit ThirdPlaces is built around. Christopher Caen’s trust layer for enterprise context sits next to the requirement that people control where their conversations go. Raj Singh can show building and shipping with AI to professionals and students who need to see the work done. Networking runs 5:00–6:00 and 7:10–8:00, which is enough time for introductions. The showcase is selected for the audience and is not pay-to-play, which matches featuring tools that serve the people in the room.

The outcomes above still require four things this draft leaves out.

**The exercise.** The opening promises hands-on techniques. From 6:10 to 7:10 the agenda is an overview, a panel with Q&A, three lightning talks, and showcase introductions. The goals need a block in which every attendee runs one method on a task or a conversation they brought.

**The three groups, named.** The draft addresses people becoming superhuman, and “AI Natives.” The goals need the invitation to name experienced professionals, students and new graduates, and career changers, and to say what each leaves with. CalMU’s programs, practitioner faculty, and campuses are the next step for students, veterans, and career changers, and they have to be visible. The measured productivity gain from a conversational assistant is largest for novices, and novices are who the room has to be able to hold.

**Introductions by kind of work.** The draft offers people who have “great synergy.” The goals need the match to be a peer in the same occupation, someone already hired into the industry a student is entering, or a path into a CalMU program.

**The choice of where the AI runs.** The showcase asks for tools that help people become superhuman and that are great for the audience. The goals need a featured tool to be usable on real work and demonstrable on a phone, on a home computer the person controls, or in the cloud.

For this first event, keep the short overview, the panel, and the networking. Add the exercise, name the three groups and what each leaves holding, make the introductions by kind of work or by program path, and hold the showcase to the choice of where the AI runs.
