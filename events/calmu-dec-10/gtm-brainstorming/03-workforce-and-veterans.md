# Plan C — Workforce and veterans

Get to 300 by asking the organizations whose job is already to help people learn a hireable skill. They refer clients, post the evening on a calendar they control, and leave a flyer where someone is already waiting for an appointment. The signup is a decision a job seeker or a veteran makes, usually after a counselor says it is worth an evening.

This plan reaches the people a campus blast misses: career changers, veterans using education benefits, and adults in a job center or an adult school. It reaches 300 only if the high-volume partners actually distribute it. Those partners are San José Public Library’s SJPL Works, work2future, and Silicon Valley Adult Education. Counselor word of mouth, the county veterans office, and campus veterans centers are the right relationships and the wrong math. Together they might produce 80 registrations. The library, the job-center email, and the adult school are what make up the rest.

If on Friday, November 21 fewer than two of those three have a send or a posting date, this plan will not hit 300. Move the gap onto Plan A or Plan B that day. Keep every workforce and veteran partner who said yes. The evening needs their audience even when they are not the whole 300.

## Who it signs up

Adults changing careers, veterans and eligible spouses, and people a public agency is already coaching toward work. Students appear where a campus veterans office forwards the note. Experienced professionals appear only if they are in a job center or a library jobs program. Do not expect a finance or consulting crowd from this plan.

The offer they will forward is a free skill workshop. CalMU’s programs are a next step for people who ask on the way out. Leading with enrollment, GI Bill optimization, or a degree pitch is how a veterans office says no. The VA’s own clinicians are a referral point for someone who asks about work or school. They are not a marketing channel.

## Where the registrations leak

A job-center client who registers often needs a reminder and a reason to spend a Thursday night. The reason in the paragraph is concrete: one method, used on their own situation, and a person to meet. A flyer that says “AI networking” will sit on the counter.

Veterans offices are careful about education marketers. The ask stays a free workshop and a flyer. The moment the email leads with accelerated degrees, the conversation becomes a vendor pitch and the forward dies.

The Vet Center and the VA clinic will not produce a counted block of registrations, and pressing them will cost the county relationship. The county office is the veteran door that can actually mention the event all three weeks.

Spanish-language distribution is part of the adult-school and job-center yield, not an extra. If the flyer and the Luma page are English only, cut the SVAE number in half and treat 300 as out of reach unless Plan A is covering the difference.
