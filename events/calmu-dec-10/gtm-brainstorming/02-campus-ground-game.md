# Plan B — Campus ground game

Get to 300 by being on campus while class is still meeting. A student hears about the evening in a room, a club meeting, or a line at a table, and registers on Luma before they walk away. Career-center staff can help, and the plan does not wait on them.

The clock is the academic calendar. San José State’s last day of instruction is Monday, December 7. Finals run December 9–11 and 14–15, and the event is Thursday, December 10, an exam night. San José City College ends its semester that same day, with English finals on December 4. Thanksgiving removes November 25–29. The only full recruiting days are November 18–24 and December 1–4.

## Who it signs up

Students and new graduates within a short transit ride of the room: San José State first, then San José City College, Evergreen Valley, Mission, and De Anza. Santa Clara University is a second-day campus, not the first. Working professionals and career changers who are not on these campuses will not be reached. Pair this plan with Plan C if that mix matters for the room.

International students are part of the class announcements, in the same sentence as everyone else. The practice is something they can show an employer. The announcement does not talk about visas.

Fifteen announcements at eight registrations each is the spine. If instructors will not give up two minutes, the table has to do more, and tables rarely beat a professor saying “take out your phone.”

Eight registrations from a class of forty is a 20% hand-raise. That is plausible for a free, specific evening and weak for a vague one. The person at the front needs the date, the hour, and the Luma QR on a slide or a printed half-sheet.

## People and permission

Recruit eight campus leads by Thursday, November 19. Two at San José State, one each at San José City College, Evergreen, Mission, and De Anza, one at Santa Clara, one floater who can cover a no-show. Pay them in a way you can say out loud: a stipend for the week, or a clear host role the night of the event. A lead with no reason to text people on a Sunday will not.

Each lead does four things:

1. Gets two instructors to give two minutes, or gets a club president to forward.
2. Stands at a table for two hours with the QR and a one-line sign.
3. Posts the paragraph in the group chats they actually have.
4. Texts people who scanned and did not finish. Luma will not show the unfinished ones, so the lead keeps a tally sheet of names they personally asked.

Ask the department office, not a dean, for the class announcement. A lecturer in an intro information-systems or career course is the right room. A 400-person lecture is the wrong room: the QR gets lost and the instructor is harder to reach.

For clubs, the SOPP specialist Patrick Harris (patrick.harris@sjsu.edu) can introduce the SJSU orgs. Use that introduction if it arrives Wednesday or Thursday. If it has not arrived by Friday morning, the leads write the club presidents directly. Club contacts are public on the student-org directory.

Veterans on campus: SJSU Veterans Resource Center, veterans@sjsu.edu, Student Union 1500, open late on weeknights. Ask to leave half-sheets and to have two minutes at the next gathering. At the community colleges, ask for the veterans certifying official or the veterans resource center by that name at the front desk. De Anza’s center is the one transfer students already know; use it. Do not guess at a staff name.

## What the lead says, out loud

About 40 seconds.

> Thursday, December 10, 5 to 8, in San Jose. It’s free. You bring one piece of work — a class project, something from a job, a problem you want to think through. You use one AI method on it, and you meet someone who already works in the field you’re trying to enter. The panel includes a Mozilla product lead and people who use AI on real work every day. I’m putting the link in the chat. Register before you leave the room. It is the evening of December 10, which is finals week here, so only register if that night is actually open.

The last sentence is doing real work. A signup from someone who has an exam that night inflates Luma and empties the room.

The half-sheet is six lines: Free. Dec 10, 5:00–8:00 p.m. San Jose. Use AI on your own work. Meet someone already hired in your field. QR. calmu’s name small, at the bottom, as the host.

## Week by week

**Tuesday, November 17.** House-list email goes out. Write the eight leads. Email eight SJSU instructors and four at the city colleges. Subject line: “Two minutes in class this week — free workshop Dec 10.”

**Wednesday–Thursday, November 18–19.** Leads confirmed. First announcements. First table is Thursday or Friday, not next week. A table on November 19 is worth more than a better-looking table on December 3.

**Friday, November 20.** Count announcements actually delivered, not emails sent to instructors. Target: 40 registrations, at least five announcements done, one table done.

**Monday–Tuesday, November 23–24.** Heaviest campus days of the whole plan. San José State is in session. Mission College is open and closes November 25. San José City College is the priority on these two days because their students disappear for a longer break. Schedule the SJCC table and announcements here, not during the week of December 1. Their English finals are December 4.

**Wednesday, November 25 through Sunday, November 29.** No tabling. Leads post in group chats on Wednesday morning so the post is at the top going into the break, then they stop. Personal texts to people who said they would register are fine. New instructor asks can wait until December 1; offices are empty.

**Monday–Thursday, December 1–4.** Second wave of announcements in sections that have not heard it. One more SJSU table. De Anza or Mission, whichever was slower in week one, gets the visit this week. Santa Clara org posts go out Monday.

**Friday, December 5 through Monday, December 7.** Last announcements only in classes that already said yes. No new campuses. December 7 is the last day a San José State student is in a regular class.

**Tuesday, December 8.** Close the count. Leads text everyone they personally registered: what to bring, and a reply if December 10 now conflicts with an exam. That text protects the room. It is not a new signup channel.

## Where the registrations leak

Finals week is the leak that matters. A student who registers on November 20 and then sees an exam on December 10 will no-show. The announcement says that out loud, and the December 8 text asks them to release the seat if the night is no longer real. Luma should stay open. The host should know, by December 9, how many registrants still say they are coming.

Tabling without a talk track collects emails and not registrations. The person at the table opens Luma on the student’s phone and stands there until the button is pressed. A QR that people photograph “for later” is a lost signup.

Club posts on a Friday afternoon of Thanksgiving week get buried. Wednesday morning, November 25, is the last post worth making before the break. December 1 is the next one.
