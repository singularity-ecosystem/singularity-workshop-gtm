# Singularity Workshop

**December 10** · with California Miramar University (CalMU)

Bring a task you own, or a conversation you want to think through. You will use one method on it, meet someone who already works that way, and leave with a next step you can name.

The evening is for experienced professionals, students and new graduates, and career changers. CalMU already reaches these groups from San Diego, San Jose, and La Puente, and online. A short picture of the tools opens the program, only long enough to set up the exercise. Then a panel of people who use AI on real work every day. Then everyone in the room runs the method. Networking is long enough for an introduction that matches the work.

## Who this evening is for

**Experienced professionals** in legal, finance, consulting, operations, software, and other professional work. You take a task you own through a tool and finish the part the tool cannot: judgment, clients, and exceptions. You meet peers who use AI on that kind of work, and you see a way to keep the practice around a job.

**Students and new graduates**, including international students in STEM-designated programs and veterans using education benefits. You leave with a practice you can show an employer, and with an introduction to someone already hired into the industry you are entering. For international students, that practice is what a STEM-designated degree has to look like while Optional Practical Training seats narrow.

**Career changers**, including veterans moving into work that is still hiring. You try, once, a skill the growing roles hire for, and you can name it afterward. You meet a practitioner, or a path into CalMU’s accelerated hybrid programs in business and technology.

Practitioner faculty and working students in the room can make the introduction specific: a peer in the same occupation, someone already in the industry a student is entering, or a program path.

## What you leave holding

- A method you have already used on your own work or your own conversation.
- An introduction matched to that work, that industry, or a CalMU program.
- A next step. Students, veterans, and career changers can see CalMU’s accelerated hybrid programs in business and technology, taught by practitioner faculty on the San Diego, San Jose, and La Puente campuses and online. Working professionals can see how to keep the practice around a job.
- The choice of which AI you use and where it runs: on your phone with no internet, on a home computer you control, or in the cloud.

Online students and online professionals are who that practice is for after the room.

## Panel

**Raj Singh**, VP of New Products at Mozilla. Four-time founder, with exits to Mozilla, Salesforce, Cookpad, and WMG. Twice an entrepreneur in residence at SRI, he turned deep research into venture-backed products and companies. Raj builds with AI, finds product-market fit, and ships. He will show professionals and students what building and shipping with AI looks like on real work.

**Christopher Caen**, COO and co-founder at JLINC. For over thirty years he has worked on the client side and the agency side for companies including Sun Microsystems, Cisco, Informix, and Oracle. For the last two years he has used that cybersecurity experience to secure AI for enterprises. JLINC builds the trust layer for enterprise context and agentic AI: where a conversation goes, and who decides.

**Chris Heuer**, on human-centered AI and the future of work. Chris uses AI as a daily thought partner and teaches others to do the same, the subject of his upcoming book *Conversations With My AI: How to Work With Your New Thought Partner*. He leads Ecosystem Development at The-AIOS, hosts the CommunityAssistant.ai podcast, and convened the dialogue behind the H-Corp Manifesto, a community-owned framework for human-centered organizations in the AI era.

## Showcase

Ten established companies and startups. Each gets a high-top table, room for a poster, and a short demonstration. A company earns the place when its tool helps with real work or ordinary conversation, and when that tool can be shown on a phone, on a home computer the person controls, or in the cloud. The audience is the test. You choose the AI and where it runs.
